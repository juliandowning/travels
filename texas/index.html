The Plan

Three States. Three Legs.

A wedding in the Texas Hill Country opens a road trip through some of America’s most musical, culinary and atmospheric cities — finishing on Beale Street in Memphis. Or cut the trip short after New Orleans if 14 nights is too much — that’s a natural break point.

✦
Leg 1 — Texas (Nights 1–5)
Austin → Marble Falls · Hill Country · Will & Kylie’s wedding
Arrive into Austin for a day in the city — live music on 6th Street, breakfast tacos, the Colorado River. Then drive 90 minutes northwest into the Hill Country for Marble Falls, the wedding weekend, and the kind of Texas that doesn’t make it onto postcards: limestone bluffs, wildflowers, wineries, and a proper slow pace.
🌊
Leg 2 — Louisiana (Nights 6–9)
Natchez overnight → New Orleans · Swamp · Jazz · Food
Drive east through Mississippi with an overnight in Natchez — antebellum mansions on a bluff above the river, genuinely atmospheric. Then New Orleans: three nights for the French Quarter, a Honey Island swamp tour with alligators (October is perfect timing), Creole food, and jazz till late. This leg alone justifies the trip.
♫
Leg 3 — Tennessee (Nights 10–14)
Nashville → Memphis · Country music · Graceland · BBQ
Up through Tennessee for the music road trip finale. Nashville for honky tonks and hot chicken, then Memphis for Graceland, Beale Street blues, and the best BBQ ribs in the world. Fly home from Memphis.
✎ Decision Point — After New Orleans (Night 9)
If 14 nights feels like a stretch, New Orleans is the natural cut-off. It’s a major hub with direct transatlantic flights, the trip has a complete arc by that point, and Nashville/Memphis can always anchor a future trip.
✈ Flights
In: Boston (BOS) → Austin (AUS) — direct on American or Southwest, ~3.5 hrs

Out (full trip): Memphis (MEM) → London Heathrow — usually one stop (Atlanta or Charlotte). Delta via ATL is the easiest connection.

Out (if cutting at NOLA): New Orleans (MSY) → London Heathrow — BA or Virgin via New York, or Delta via ATL.
🚗 Driving
Hire a car in Austin, return in Memphis (or NOLA if cutting early). One-way hire is straightforward for major US operators — book early, prices vary wildly. The drives are all highway-comfortable with things to stop for en route:

Austin → Marble Falls: ~65 miles, ~90 mins (northwest on US-183 / TX-71)
Marble Falls → Natchez: ~430 miles, ~6.5 hrs — break the drive in San Antonio or stop for lunch
Natchez → New Orleans: 170 miles, ~2.5 hrs
New Orleans → Nashville: 480 miles, ~7 hrs — long day, see below
Nashville → Memphis: 210 miles, ~3 hrs
NOLA → Nashville drive: This is the one long haul. Break it in Mississippi — Jackson is the obvious midpoint — or take a direct flight (Southwest flies it for under $100 and takes 1 hr 10 mins). Flying frees up a day for Nashville, which is worth having.
🌡 October in the South
Ideal timing. Texas Hill Country: 70–80°F, clear skies, start of fall colour. Louisiana: hot season breaking, humidity dropping, still warm (mid-70s). Swamp tours at their best — alligators active, mosquitoes less so. Tennessee: crisp and colourful, 60–70°F. Outdoor dining everywhere is excellent this time of year.
👕
What to Wear
Layers. Texas days can hit 80°F but October evenings in Nashville dip to 50°F. A light jacket is essential. Smart-casual for Nashville & Memphis bars. One outfit for the wedding. Comfortable walking shoes throughout — New Orleans streets are uneven.
📸
Don’t Miss
Book Honey Island Swamp tour in advance — small group sizes. Graceland tickets online to skip queues. Reserve at Commander’s Palace (NOLA) a week ahead minimum. Beale Street is walk-in, Ryman Auditorium shows sell out.
🚘
Car Hire Tips
Full-size SUV or large sedan — three people, luggage, and long highway miles. Book one-way Austin → Memphis (or NOLA). Enterprise and Hertz are the most reliable for one-ways. Consider a SiriusXM radio trial for the road.
🍽
Food Musts
Austin: breakfast tacos & Franklin BBQ (queue early). Marble Falls: Flat Creek Estate Winery or a lakeside lunch. NOLA: beignets at Café Du Monde, po’boys, Commander’s Palace brunch. Nashville: hot chicken at Prince’s. Memphis: Rendezvous ribs or Central BBQ.
I
Texas — Austin & the Hill Country
Nights 1–5 · Arrive Austin · Marble Falls · Will & Kylie’s Wedding
Day 1

Austin Arrives

Land, decompress, and ease into the city at your own pace. Austin is one of the most laid-back arrivals you can make — no grand agenda, just good food, live music in the evening, and a proper night’s sleep.

Afternoon

Arrive Austin-Bergstrom (AUS). Collect hire car. Check in and drop bags. The drive from the airport into downtown is 15 minutes — no stress.

Late Afternoon

South Congress Avenue (SoCo). A gentle wander — independent shops, vintage stores, food trucks. Grab a margarita and a table outside. Jo’s Coffee has the famous “I Love You So Much” mural worth a stop.

Dusk

Congress Avenue Bridge bat colony. One of the great free spectacles in America — 1.5 million Mexican free-tailed bats emerge from under the bridge at dusk in a continuous dark ribbon for 20–30 minutes. October is still prime season before they migrate south. Get there 20 minutes early and watch from the bridge or the grassy bank below. Completely unmissable.

Evening

6th Street or Rainey Street. Austin’s live music scene is genuinely world-class. Wander the honky tonks and dive bars on 6th Street, or the converted bungalows of Rainey Street for something slightly more grown-up. Grab dinner at Uchi (high-end Japanese) or keep it casual with tacos anywhere.

Smart Choice
Hotel Van Buren
Austin Downtown
Boutique hotel on West 6th, sharp design, rooftop pool. Walking distance to everything. Great base for two or three nights without needing the car in the city.
From ~$200/night
The Landmark
The Driskill
6th Street, Austin Downtown
Austin’s most storied hotel — built in 1886 by cattle baron Jesse Driskill, ornate Romanesque lobby, stained glass, longhorn busts. Right on 6th Street. The Driskill Bar is one of the best bars in Texas. If you’re spending two nights in Austin, this is the one to do it in.
From ~$260/night
Day 2

Austin Proper

The full Austin day. Breakfast tacos, the Capitol, the river, and live music again in the evening. This is one of America’s most enjoyable cities to just wander.

Morning

Breakfast tacos at Veracruz All Natural or Tacodeli — a genuine Austin institution. Then Texas State Capitol — worth going in, the rotunda is impressive and it’s free.

Midday

Franklin Barbecue if you’re committed (queue from 8am, opens at 11, sells out by 1pm — it’s worth it once in a lifetime). Or try La Barbecue for a shorter wait and equally serious brisket.

Afternoon

Barton Springs Pool — a spring-fed swimming pool in Zilker Park, one of Austin’s great pleasures. 68°F year-round, outdoors, beautiful. Then a walk or bike along the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake.

Evening

Continental Club or Stubb’s for live music. Dinner on South Congress or East Austin — the east side has the best new restaurants right now. Try Launderette, Emmer & Rye, or Comedor for something special.

Art detour: The Blanton Museum of Art on the UT campus is excellent and free on Thursdays. The “Ellsworth Kelly: Austin” building in the grounds is one of the most quietly extraordinary things in America.
Day 3

Into the Hill Country

Ninety minutes northwest of Austin, the landscape shifts completely — limestone bluffs, cedar and pecan, the Highland Lakes chain, and small towns at a proper Texas pace. Marble Falls is the wedding base, beautifully set on Lake Marble Falls with the Hill Country all around.

Morning

Check out of Austin. Drive northwest ~65 miles to Marble Falls — about 90 minutes on US-183 and TX-71. Easy, scenic drive through the Hill Country. No detours needed: the road itself is the experience.

Afternoon

Arrive Marble Falls. Check in, decompress. Marble Falls sits on the Colorado River arm of Lake LBJ and Lake Marble Falls — genuinely beautiful Hill Country lake country. Stroll the lakefront, or head out to Flat Creek Estate Winery or Fall Creek Vineyards for an afternoon tasting — both are excellent and close by.

Evening

Quiet dinner in Marble Falls or at the venue. The next two days are wedding weekend — save energy.

Days 4 & 5

Wedding Weekend

The reason you’re here. Two days anchored around Will & Kylie’s wedding — with the Hill Country as the backdrop and the freedom to enjoy the day before and after at your own pace.

💌
Will & Kylie
The Retreat at Balcones Springs · Marble Falls, Texas · 27–28 October 2027
Day before the wedding: Keep it relaxed. A winery visit, a walk around the Balcones Springs grounds, or just a long lakeside lunch. You want to arrive at the wedding refreshed, not frazzled from sightseeing.
Day after the wedding: A slow morning is earned. Brunch somewhere, maybe a final Hill Country drive before heading east toward Natchez the following day.

II
Louisiana — Natchez & New Orleans
Nights 6–9 · Mississippi River · Swamp · French Quarter · Jazz
Day 6

Natchez on the Bluff

The drive east from Blanco takes most of the day. Natchez is the overnight stop — one of the oldest European settlements in the Mississippi valley, sitting on a dramatic bluff above the river. Antebellum mansions, Spanish moss, and a completely different atmosphere from Texas.

Morning

Check out of Marble Falls. Drive east ~430 miles to Natchez. Break somewhere in San Antonio or further along in Louisiana for lunch. The drive is highway all the way — put SiriusXM on and enjoy it.

Evening

Arrive Natchez. Walk to the Grand Village bluff overlook for the Mississippi sunset — the river is vast here, and the view explains everything about why this town mattered. Dinner at Pearl Street Pasta or Cotton Alley Cafe, both reliable and atmospheric.

One Night, Make It Count
Monmouth Historic Inn
Natchez, MS
An antebellum mansion turned inn — 1818 Greek Revival, 30 acres, evening cocktails on the verandah. Wildly atmospheric for one night. This is exactly what Natchez is for.
From ~$200/night
Practical Choice
Natchez Grand Hotel
Natchez, MS
Central, comfortable, easy parking. A good base if you’re arriving late and just need somewhere solid to sleep before pushing on to New Orleans in the morning.
From ~$120/night
Days 7–9

New Orleans

Three nights in one of the world’s great cities. New Orleans doesn’t do half measures — the food is extraordinary, the music is everywhere, and the swamp is right on the doorstep. October is the best month to come.

Day 7

Arrive NOLA. Drive from Natchez is 2.5 hrs, easy morning run. Check in, then walk the French Quarter: Jackson Square, St Louis Cathedral, Royal Street galleries, Bourbon Street (once, briefly). Lunch at Central Grocery for a muffuletta — the original and still the best. Evening: Frenchmen Street for live jazz in small clubs, which is far better than Bourbon Street.

Day 8

Honey Island Swamp Tour — half day, morning departure. One of the best wildlife experiences available from any American city. October is ideal: alligators active and numerous, humidity dropping, Spanish moss at its most atmospheric. Book Dr Wagner’s Honey Island Swamp Tours in advance — small boats, knowledgeable guides. Afternoon: Garden District walk, Commander’s Palace for a legendary late lunch or early dinner (book ahead).

Day 9

Slow morning. Beignets at Café Du Monde, then the NOMA (New Orleans Museum of Art) in City Park if you want culture, or a streetcar ride up St Charles Avenue for architecture. Final evening: Arnaud’s or Galatoire’s for classic Creole — jacket required, worth it.

✎ Decision Point — Cut the trip here?
New Orleans has direct transatlantic options (BA and Virgin via New York; Delta via Atlanta). If 9 nights feels like a natural ending, this is the cleanest break. The trip is complete as a story: Texas wedding, Mississippi River, Louisiana in full. Nashville and Memphis are a bonus chapter, not a requirement.

III
Tennessee — Nashville & Memphis
Nights 10–14 · Country Music · Graceland · Beale Street · BBQ
Days 10 & 11

Nashville

The Music City leg. Two nights for honky tonks, hot chicken, and the Ryman Auditorium — the “Mother Church of Country Music” and one of the best small venues in the world. Nashville has changed enormously in recent years but the bones are still great.

NOLA to Nashville: Strongly consider flying this leg — Southwest or Delta do it in just over an hour for under $100. The drive is 7+ hours and doesn’t add much to the trip. Flying gives you an extra day in Nashville and a painless transition.
Day 10

Arrive Nashville. Lower Broadway honky tonks for the full immersion — Robert’s Western World is the real deal among the tourist bars. Afternoon: Country Music Hall of Fame — genuinely excellent museum, full day if you’re into it or a couple of hours if not. Evening: Ryman Auditorium show if anything is on (check listings early — it sells out).

Day 11

Hot chicken at Prince’s Nashville Hot Chicken for lunch — the original, no debate. Morning: The Gulch neighbourhood for coffee and the “What Lifts You” angel wings mural. Afternoon: Belle Meade historic plantation and winery, or The Parthenon in Centennial Park (a full-scale replica, genuinely odd and worth seeing). Evening: 12South neighbourhood for dinner — Burger Up or The Silly Goose for something relaxed.

Downtown Buzz

Days 12–14

Memphis & Graceland

The finale. Memphis is rawer and more lived-in than Nashville, and better for it. Graceland is unmissable even if you’re not an Elvis fan. And the BBQ here is a legitimate argument for flying specifically to Memphis and eating nothing else.

Day 12

Drive Nashville → Memphis, 3 hours, easy. Graceland in the afternoon — allow 2–3 hours for the full tour including the Jungle Room, the grave, and the car museum. It’s kitsch and moving in equal measure. Evening: Beale Street for blues bars — B.B. King’s Blues Club or Blues City Cafe. Dinner at Gus’s Fried Chicken (queue, worth it).

Day 13

National Civil Rights Museum at the Lorraine Motel — powerful and important, half a day minimum. Lunch: Rendezvous for dry-rubbed ribs (down an alley, cash only, iconic) or Central BBQ for pulled pork. Afternoon: Sun Studio tour — where Elvis, Johnny Cash, Jerry Lee Lewis and Roy Orbison all recorded. Genuinely spine-tingling. Evening: a last look at Beale Street or a quiet dinner.

Day 14

Fly home. Memphis International (MEM) has connections via Atlanta (Delta) or Charlotte (American) to Heathrow. Typical routing: MEM → ATL → LHR. Book early for good fares and seat selection on the transatlantic leg.
